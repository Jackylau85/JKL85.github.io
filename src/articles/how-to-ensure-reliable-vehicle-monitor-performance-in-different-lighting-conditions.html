How to Ensure Reliable Vehicle Monitor Performance in Different Lighting Conditions

Practical considerations for maintaining clear visibility and reliable vehicle monitor performance in sunlight, changing light conditions, nighttime operation, and high-temperature environments.

Category: Monitors

Publication date: 2026-10-19

Introduction

In commercial vehicles, the performance of a vehicle monitor depends not only on the product itself but also on the working environment, installation position, and operating conditions.

For example, a forklift working outdoors may be exposed to direct sunlight, making camera images difficult to see. A truck entering or leaving a tunnel may experience rapid changes in ambient light, while a monitor that is too bright during nighttime reversing may reflect off the windshield and interfere with the driver's view of the surroundings.

These problems cannot always be solved by simply increasing screen brightness. To ensure reliable performance under different lighting conditions, several factors need to be considered, including installation position, sun protection, optical performance, brightness adjustment, and heat dissipation.

01 | Strong Sunlight: Optimize Installation and Sun Protection First

For forklifts, construction machinery, port equipment, and other commercial vehicles that frequently operate outdoors, direct sunlight is a common cause of poor screen visibility.

When sunlight shines directly onto the screen, reflections can make camera images difficult to distinguish. Even a monitor with high brightness may not completely overcome this problem.

Therefore, when a monitor is difficult to see in strong sunlight, the first step should be to check its installation position rather than immediately replacing it with a higher-brightness model.

First, avoid direct sunlight whenever possible.

If the vehicle structure and driver's field of view allow, adjust the monitor's installation position and tilt angle to reduce direct exposure to sunlight.

This is particularly important for forklifts, construction machinery, and outdoor equipment without enclosed cabs, where monitors are more exposed to direct sunlight.

Second, adjust the screen angle to reduce reflections.

The monitor's installation angle affects both viewing comfort and how external light reflects off the screen.

If the screen faces a strong light source, reflections may still interfere with visibility even when the monitor has sufficient brightness. Adjusting the screen orientation and tilt angle can sometimes improve viewing conditions significantly.

Third, consider installing a sunshade when necessary.

If the vehicle structure limits the available installation positions and direct sunlight cannot be avoided, a sunshade may help.

A properly designed sunshade reduces direct light reaching the screen. It can also reduce the additional heat load caused by solar radiation on the screen and housing, helping lower the risk of high-temperature-related brightness reduction, display abnormalities, or thermal protection.

However, the sunshade must be designed carefully. It should not obstruct the driver's field of view, touchscreen operation, or physical buttons. It must also withstand vehicle vibration without restricting airflow or trapping heat around the monitor.

After optimizing the installation position and sun protection, evaluate the monitor's optical performance.

If the screen is still difficult to see in strong sunlight, consider its brightness, surface reflectivity, contrast, and cover-lens design.

When selecting a monitor, do not rely on the advertised brightness rating alone. Actual outdoor visibility should be evaluated in relation to the installation angle, direction of sunlight, and driver's viewing position.

02 | Changing Light Conditions: Consider Brightness Control and Sensor Placement

Some commercial vehicles do not operate under constant lighting conditions.

For example, trucks may frequently enter and leave tunnels, port equipment may move between open areas and warehouses, and forklifts may travel between outdoor loading zones and indoor storage areas.

In these situations, ambient light can change significantly within a short period.

If the monitor maintains a fixed brightness level, the screen may become difficult to see when the environment gets brighter. When the vehicle enters a darker area, the same brightness level may become excessive.

Depending on the vehicle's operating conditions, either manual or automatic brightness adjustment may be appropriate.

Manual brightness adjustment is suitable for relatively stable lighting conditions.

If a vehicle operates mainly in a fixed work area where lighting changes very little, the driver can adjust the brightness as needed. Automatic brightness control may not be necessary.

Automatic brightness adjustment is more useful when lighting conditions change frequently.

With a light sensor, the monitor can automatically adjust its brightness according to detected ambient light, reducing the need for frequent manual adjustments.

However, automatic brightness control does not guarantee ideal visibility under all conditions.

The sensor's location, detection direction, and response speed can all affect performance. For example, if the sensor is blocked or detects light that does not accurately represent the conditions around the screen, the brightness adjustment may be inappropriate.

If the response is too slow, the screen may remain too bright after the vehicle enters a dark area. If the adjustment is too sensitive, brightness may fluctuate repeatedly in response to minor changes in light.

Therefore, when selecting a monitor with automatic brightness control, check not only whether it has a light sensor but also whether brightness changes smoothly and respond appropriately to real operating conditions.

03 | Nighttime Operation: Prevent Screen Reflections from Interfering with the Driver's View

At night, the challenge is not simply whether the screen is bright enough. The driver must be able to see both the camera image on the monitor and the actual surroundings outside the vehicle.

For example, when reversing at night, the driver may need to monitor the rear camera image while also watching for pedestrians, obstacles, and other vehicles nearby.

If the monitor brightness is set too high, light from the screen may reflect off the windshield, creating noticeable internal reflections. This is especially problematic when the cab is dark and the surrounding area has little ambient light. The reflected screen image can appear like a mirror image on the windshield and interfere with the driver's view of the outside environment.

For this reason, a brighter screen is not necessarily better for nighttime operation.

First, adjust the screen brightness to suit the surrounding conditions.

The brightness should be sufficient for the driver to identify important details in the camera image without creating unnecessary visual interference.

For vehicles that frequently switch between daytime and nighttime operation, automatic brightness adjustment may be useful. For vehicles operating mainly in a consistent nighttime environment, manual adjustment may be sufficient.

Second, check the installation if reflections remain after reducing brightness.

Check the monitor's position, screen orientation, and the driver's viewing angle. The reflective characteristics of the windshield should also be considered.

Sometimes, the problem is not the monitor's brightness itself but the relative position of the screen and windshield, which allows the screen's reflection to enter the driver's field of view.

The goal of nighttime optimization is therefore not simply to dim the screen. It is to maintain a clear and usable camera image while minimizing interference with the driver's ability to observe the surroundings outside the vehicle.

04 | High Temperatures and Long Operating Hours: Pay Attention to Heat Dissipation and Stability

For commercial vehicles operating outdoors, strong sunlight often comes with high temperatures.

When a monitor is exposed to direct sunlight for extended periods, the screen and housing may absorb additional heat. If the ambient temperature is already high and ventilation around the installation area is poor, the internal temperature of the device may rise further.

This can affect more than viewing comfort. Excessive heat may cause brightness reduction, display abnormalities, or activation of the monitor's thermal protection mechanism.

When selecting a vehicle monitor, it is therefore important to consider not only outdoor visibility but also the specified operating temperature range, thermal design, and stability during extended operation.

For forklifts, construction machinery, and port equipment that operate outdoors for long periods, pay particular attention to the following factors.

  • Check the operating temperature range. The monitor's rated operating temperature should cover the environmental conditions the vehicle is likely to encounter. Additional heat from direct sunlight should also be considered rather than relying on ambient air temperature alone.
  • Consider ventilation around the installation area. Leave adequate space around the monitor for heat dissipation. Avoid installation arrangements that block airflow.
  • Use sunshades carefully. A sunshade can reduce additional heat from solar radiation, but a poorly designed structure may restrict airflow. Sun protection and heat dissipation must be considered together.
  • Check performance during extended operation. Run the monitor for a sustained period under appropriate operating conditions and observe whether the screen becomes dim, the display becomes unstable, or other abnormalities appear.

It is important to remember that installing a sunshade does not guarantee that a monitor will never overheat. High ambient temperatures, continuous operation, and poor ventilation can still cause thermal problems.

05 | Test the Monitor on the Actual Vehicle, Not Only on a Workbench

A monitor that performs well in a laboratory or on a workbench may not deliver the same results after installation in a vehicle.

Actual lighting direction, driver position, windshield reflections, vehicle vibration, and heat dissipation conditions may differ significantly from the test environment.

Before finalizing the monitor model and installation arrangement, it is advisable to evaluate the system under real operating conditions.

The following situations deserve particular attention.

  • Test under strong midday sunlight. Check whether the driver can clearly identify important details in the camera image when sunlight shines directly onto the screen or reaches it from different directions.
  • Test under side lighting. Check whether sunlight entering from the side causes noticeable reflections. Evaluate whether changing the screen angle or adding a sunshade improves visibility.
  • Test transitions between bright and dark environments. Simulate the vehicle entering and leaving a warehouse, tunnel, or other area with significant changes in ambient light. Observe whether the screen brightness adapts appropriately.
  • Test nighttime operation. From the actual driver's position, check whether the screen is too bright or produces distracting reflections on the windshield. At the same time, confirm that the camera image remains clear enough to identify relevant details.
  • Test during extended outdoor operation. Within the monitor's specified operating conditions, observe its display stability after prolonged use. Also check whether the installation position, sunshade, and mounting bracket affect ventilation or normal operation.

These tests help determine whether a visibility problem is caused by the monitor itself or by installation position, sun protection, reflections, or thermal design. This can prevent unnecessary replacements based solely on poor visibility in strong sunlight.

Conclusion: Choose the Right Monitor, Install It Correctly, and Maintain It Properly

When selecting a vehicle monitor, do not compare products based only on brightness and resolution. Start by considering the vehicle's actual operating environment.

For vehicles that frequently operate outdoors in strong sunlight, prioritize outdoor visibility and plan the installation position, screen orientation, and sun protection in advance. For vehicles that operate continuously during both day and night, check whether the monitor offers suitable brightness adjustment to prevent excessive screen brightness from interfering with the driver's view of the surroundings at night.

After installation, evaluate the display under actual working conditions. Confirm that the driver can clearly identify the camera image, minimize overheating caused by direct sunlight, and ensure that the mounting arrangement does not interfere with heat dissipation.

During daily operation, clean the screen regularly, check that the sunshade and mounting bracket remain secure, inspect connection points, and watch for abnormal brightness, dimming, or unstable operation. Vehicles working for long periods in high-temperature, dusty, or outdoor environments require particular attention to routine inspection and maintenance.

Consider the operating environment when selecting the monitor, optimize its position and sun protection during installation, and maintain it through regular cleaning and inspection. These steps help ensure good visibility and reliable performance under different lighting conditions.
