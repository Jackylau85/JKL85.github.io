Is a Bigger Monitor Always Better for Commercial Vehicles?

Screen size alone does not determine how clearly drivers can see hazards. The right monitor should help drivers recognize important objects while fitting the vehicle's installation space and viewing requirements.

Category: Monitors

Introduction: Bigger Is Not Always Better

When choosing a rear-view monitor for a commercial vehicle, buyers often start by comparing screen sizes. Is 7 inches enough? Would a 10.1-inch monitor be better? If the budget allows, should they simply choose the larger screen?

These are reasonable questions, but they overlook something more important.

Drivers do not need a bigger screen just for the sake of having one. They need an image that helps them recognize hazards clearly and in time.

Screen size is a means to achieve that goal, not the goal itself.

1. Does a Bigger Screen Make Hazards Easier to See?

Imagine a truck reversing while the driver uses a rear-view camera to check for pedestrians and obstacles behind the vehicle.

Replacing a 7-inch monitor with a 10.1-inch model increases the display area. But does that automatically make pedestrians easier to identify?

Not necessarily.

If the camera captures limited detail or the input signal has insufficient resolution, a larger monitor cannot recreate information that was never captured in the first place.

On the other hand, if the camera provides sufficient image detail but the original screen is too small, a larger display may make important objects easier to distinguish.

The key is to consider the camera image, the display and the driver's actual viewing conditions together.

Making an image larger does not necessarily add detail. A larger display does not automatically improve hazard recognition.

2. Why Screen Size Matters More When Multiple Cameras Are Used

For a system displaying a single rear-view image, the driver can focus on one full-screen view.

However, forklifts, construction machinery and other commercial vehicles may use multiple cameras to monitor different areas around the vehicle.

Consider a four-camera system. When four images are displayed simultaneously, each view occupies only part of the screen.

On a small monitor, pedestrians, vehicle edges and low obstacles may appear relatively small within each section, making them harder to distinguish.

A larger monitor can provide more display area for each camera view. However, the actual benefit also depends on image layout, input resolution, scaling and viewing distance.

This explains why a 7-inch monitor may work well in single-view mode but may be less suitable for displaying four camera views simultaneously.

Screen size should never be evaluated separately from the task the display needs to perform.

3. Why a Bigger Monitor Can Also Be the Wrong Choice

A commercial vehicle cabin is a working environment, not an ordinary office desk. The monitor must fit around the dashboard, mirrors, controls and the driver's field of view.

A truck may offer relatively generous mounting space. A forklift may have much tighter installation constraints. An excavator operator may need to monitor camera images while also controlling the machine and observing the surrounding work area.

Choosing a monitor that is too large for the intended installation may create several problems:

  • Limited mounting space: The monitor may be difficult to position properly.
  • Restricted visibility: Poor placement may obstruct part of the driver's view of the road or work area.
  • Uncomfortable viewing: The driver may need to turn their head or shift their gaze more than necessary.
  • Limited practical benefit: The screen may be larger without providing meaningfully more useful information.

In these situations, a larger monitor does not necessarily deliver a better experience.

The right monitor should display the necessary information clearly while fitting the cabin and preserving the driver's primary field of view.

4. Four Questions to Answer Before Choosing a Monitor Size

Instead of immediately asking a supplier for a larger screen, buyers should first clarify the actual application requirements.

1. What does the driver need to see?

Is the system displaying a single rear-view image, or does the driver need to monitor multiple areas around the vehicle? Different viewing tasks place different demands on the available display area.

2. How much detail can the camera provide?

If the source image lacks sufficient resolution, increasing the screen size may not solve the visibility problem. The camera, video input and monitor should be evaluated as one system.

3. Where will the monitor be installed?

The installation position and viewing distance matter as much as the screen's diagonal measurement. A monitor that looks suitable on a desk may perform differently when mounted in an actual vehicle cabin.

4. Can the driver recognize important objects under real operating conditions?

Testing should use the intended mounting position, camera layout and realistic lighting conditions. The driver should be able to distinguish relevant objects without the monitor interfering with normal driving or operating tasks.

Answering these questions provides a more reliable basis for selecting a screen size than comparing inches alone.

5. Good Monitor Selection Starts With the Problem, Not the Product

For vehicle manufacturers, aftermarket system integrators and purchasing teams, monitor selection should go beyond comparing screen sizes.

If a customer struggles to recognize obstacles in a four-camera display, the solution may involve a larger screen. It may also require a different image layout or a higher-resolution camera.

If the monitor obstructs the driver's view, increasing its size could make the problem worse.

If the image is difficult to see in bright sunlight, buyers should also evaluate display brightness, surface reflections and mounting angle rather than simply choosing a larger model.

Identify the visibility problem first. Then decide whether a larger monitor is the right solution.

This approach helps avoid unsuitable product choices and ensures that the monitor is matched to the vehicle's actual operating requirements.

Conclusion: Choose for Visibility, Not Just for Inches

There is no single monitor size that works best for every commercial vehicle.

A 7-inch monitor is not necessarily too small, and a 10.1-inch monitor is not automatically better. The right choice depends on the viewing task, installation conditions, camera system and driver's needs.

Instead of asking, "What is the best monitor size?", ask a more useful question:

"Can the driver clearly recognize the objects that matter on this screen?"

That is the real starting point for choosing the right monitor size for a commercial vehicle.
